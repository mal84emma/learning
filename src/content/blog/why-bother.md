OUTLINE (from docs/planning.md)

Introduction. Three reasons to bother learning:
1. Learning stuff lets you do and build cool things (and you should build cool things)
2. Learning and truly understanding things is deeply satisfying in itself
3. Getting good at learning helps you build even cooler things in future, more quickly

Threads to weave:
- "So you can get a job" is a dreary answer for someone with hopes beyond a corporate desk
- Looking back on (too much) education & teaching: we almost never talk about learning
  itself — what the aim is, what good methods are, how the teaching is meant to produce it
- Learning must implicitly be happening; the point of education is to enable it. So why
  isn't learning *taught*? Why do the great resources on learning end up as side projects?
- In a world where LLMs answer any question instantly, is it still worth learning?
- Goal of the project: explain what we want to achieve with learning, the stages to get
  there, the principles behind good learning, and how to learn better/smarter/quicker now
  that intelligence is cheap and learning is becoming optional

Target: ~1,000–1,250 words. Set up the narrative for "what even is learning?".
